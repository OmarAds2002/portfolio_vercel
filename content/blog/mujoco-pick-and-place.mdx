I built a 6-DOF torque-controlled pick-and-place environment in MuJoCo and trained it with PPO and SAC via Stable-Baselines3, logging everything to Weights & Biases. SAC reached a **1.0 success rate** with domain randomization; PPO worked but was noticeably seed-sensitive.

The training curves were the boring part. The interesting part was that before the policy learned to pick things up, it found four different ways to cheat — and each one was really a bug in *my* reward, not clever behavior by the agent.

## Shaping, honestly

The reward uses potential-based shaping in the sense of Ng et al. (1999), so the extra guidance never changes the optimal policy — it only makes the gradient easier to follow:

```python
# Potential-based shaping: F = gamma * phi(s') - phi(s)
def shaping(prev_state, state, gamma=0.99):
    return gamma * potential(state) - potential(prev_state)

def potential(s):
    # negative distance from gripper to object, then object to goal
    return -(dist(s.gripper, s.object) + dist(s.object, s.goal))
```

That plus a light reverse curriculum (start near the goal, back off as success climbs) is the whole scaffolding. Everything below is a story about it leaking.

## Exploit 1: drag-farming

The shaping rewarded reducing the object-to-goal distance every step. So the policy learned to pin the object against the table and *shove* it toward the goal — never lifting it, just farming the distance-delta reward forever.

**Fix:** gate the goal-distance reward on the object actually being grasped and off the surface, so sliding earns nothing.

## Exploit 2: grip-cycling

Once there was a bonus for closing the gripper on the object, the policy discovered it could open and close repeatedly to collect the "grasp" signal over and over without ever moving anything.

**Fix:** reward the *transition* into a stable grasp once, not the grasped state every step — a latch, not a faucet.

## Exploit 3: the ballistic fling

With a lift-and-place reward in place, the policy found that if it flung the object hard enough, the object would sail through the goal region for a single frame and trip the success check — no controlled placement required.

**Fix:** require the object to be near the goal *and* nearly at rest (low velocity) for the success condition, over a small dwell window.

## Exploit 4: the velocity-gate loop

The velocity gate I added for Exploit 3 created its own loophole: the policy learned to nudge the object to the edge of the goal, let it settle to trip the gate, then knock it out and back in to re-trigger.

**Fix:** make success terminal and one-shot per episode, so there's nothing to farm by re-entering.

## What it cost me, and what it taught me

Every one of these looked like a training problem — a bad curve, a stuck policy — and every one was actually a specification problem. RL doesn't optimize what you meant; it optimizes what you wrote. A torque-controlled arm with a few million environment steps is a very fast, very literal proofreader for your reward function.

## Results

- **SAC: 1.0 success** with domain randomization
- **PPO:** solved the task but seed-sensitive
- Four reward exploits found and fixed: drag-farming, grip-cycling, ballistic fling, velocity-gate loop
